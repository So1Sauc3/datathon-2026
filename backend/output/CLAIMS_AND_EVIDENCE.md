# Claims & Evidence appendix

Generated by `python -m analysis.run`. The Gilded Ace is fictional and its data synthetic; only `4_context/real_sources/` is real public data.

## Q1 Truly underperforming machines

### Q1-manager-list: The manager's bottom 10 (lowest total house win) mostly reflects where machines sit, what game settings they run and how long they were on the floor, not broken machines.

- **Table / query:** floor_day (machine_day + prepared_daily_view + game_math, on-floor rows) ranked by SUM(house_win); waterfall decomposition in analysis/q1_machines.py
- **Result:** 9 of 10 are in zone Z-B40; 8 run configs with theo hold <= 8%; partial window: M-5076, M-1120, M-1118 (retired: M-1120, M-1118); primary drivers: {'game mix': 7, 'partial window': 2, 'payout anomaly': 1}.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Bad luck: compared house win with theo using each machine's exact win variance. Downtime: availability on the list averages 99.7% vs 99.6% floor-wide.
- **Limitation:** Zone and config are partly confounded (G-HL90 only appears in Z-HL; G-NEW91 only in Z-PIT).

### Q1-payout-anomaly: M-1158 pays out on far more spins than its configured game allows, and has done so in every month; it is a lead for a paytable inspection, not proof of wrongdoing.

- **Table / query:** machine_day.payout_positive_spins / paid_spins vs game_math.hit_probability; binomial z-test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction over all 310 machines
- **Result:** hit rate 38.6% vs configured 31.5% (z = 124, q < 1e-6); hold 3.0% vs theo hold 10.0%; luck z = -4.6; $36,165 below theo over 181 days.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Luck: a luck z this extreme happens about once in 205,176 machines, and luck would not hold the hit rate steady month after month. Config change: placements shows one config for the whole window.
- **Limitation:** The hit-rate gap shows payouts differ from the published game math; it cannot say why (miscalibration, wrong paytable chip, or recording).

### Q1-luck: Apart from the payout anomaly, the spread of results versus theo is what luck alone produces.

- **Table / query:** luck_z = (house_win - theo) / sqrt(sum_bet_squared x payout_multiplier_variance), per machine
- **Result:** 20 of 310 machines beyond |z| > 2 (about 14 expected by chance); 1 remain significant after Benjamini-Hochberg at q < 0.05.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** A real machine problem would show as a hit-rate or theo gap, not only a house-win gap.
- **Limitation:** Normal approximation to the sum of many spins; very high volatility games converge slowly.

### Q1-location: Zone Z-B40 earns the least theo per available hour once its game mix is accounted for.

- **Table / query:** Indirect standardization: zone theo / sum(config floor-wide theo per available hour x hours)
- **Result:** Zone factors: Z-B40 0.65, Z-GAR 0.95, Z-HL 0.99, Z-HTL 1.01, Z-BAR 1.02, Z-PIT 1.13, Z-ENT 1.19.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Game mix: factors already divide out each config's floor-wide earning rate.
- **Limitation:** G-HL90 appears only in Z-HL, so the high-limit room's factor is close to 1 by construction and says little about that zone.

### Q1-persistence: Machine effects (theo vs location- and game-adjusted expectation) are stable between quarters.

- **Table / query:** Machine effect computed separately for Jan-Mar and Apr-Jun, machines with >= 30 days in each
- **Result:** Correlation of the two quarters' machine effects: 0.79. Persistent bottom-decile machines (excluding retired): M-1162, M-1230, M-1026, M-1008, M-1068, M-1126, M-1184, M-1255, M-1045, M-1154, M-1286, M-1006, M-1145, M-1532, M-1244, M-1094.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Noise: a machine effect driven by chance would not repeat across quarters.
- **Limitation:** Theo depends on the bets players choose, so a machine effect can reflect its neighbours or sightlines rather than the cabinet.

## Q2 Demand changes

### Q2-trend: Floor demand peaked in 2025-02 and was lowest in 2025-06; the June dip lines up with the city-wide drop in visitors and conventions.

- **Table / query:** property_day coin-in per gaming day by month; weekday-mix-adjusted index; city_visitation_monthly (LVCVA, real)
- **Result:** Coin-in per day ranged $1,324,352 (2025-06) to $1,413,478 (2025-02); weekday-adjusted index 0.985-1.048. Correlation with LVCVA visitors/day 0.49, with convention attendance 0.49 (6 months only). LVCVA visitors were down 1% to 12% year over year.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Weekday mix: the adjusted index removes months with extra weekends. Machine count: active machines stayed between 300 and 308.
- **Limitation:** LVCVA data describe all Las Vegas visitors, were published after 2025 and cannot have been known at the time; six monthly points cannot establish causation.

### Q2-weekday: Saturday brings in 1.60x the coin-in of Tuesday; weekday is the largest regular demand driver.

- **Table / query:** property_day mean coin-in by weekday, excluding event and holiday days
- **Result:** Mon $1,192,955; Tue $1,088,765; Wed $1,139,093; Thu $1,275,147; Fri $1,600,872; Sat $1,747,127; Sun $1,394,420
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Events: event and holiday days are excluded from the profile.
- **Limitation:** Averages over about 22 ordinary days per weekday.

### Q2-events: Events lift coin-in by different amounts; the property's own promotions are the evidence base for pricing a promotion weekend.

- **Table / query:** Each event day vs the mean of the same weekday within 21 days on ordinary days (property_day)
- **Result:** Super Bowl LIX weekend: +23.7% (3 days, $1,112,676 extra coin-in); College basketball tournament opening rounds: +17.8% (4 days, $1,098,175 extra coin-in); Gilded Ace Winter Slot Tournament: +17.4% (3 days, $820,312 extra coin-in); EDC Las Vegas festival weekend: +15.5% (3 days, $733,008 extra coin-in); Gilded Ace Locals Appreciation Week: +6.1% (7 days, $531,177 extra coin-in); NAB Show: +4.1% (5 days, $222,244 extra coin-in); CES: +1.6% (4 days, $70,424 extra coin-in); CinemaCon: +0.3% (4 days, $11,181 extra coin-in)
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Seasonality: the comparison days are the same weekday within three weeks.
- **Limitation:** One occurrence per event; overlapping events (CinemaCon then NAB) share comparison days.

### Q2-renovation: Closing the entrance walkway to the Back Forty (Mar 3-Apr 7) cut its play, and it has not fully recovered.

- **Table / query:** Daily ratio of Z-B40 coin-in per machine to control zones (all except Z-B40 and Z-HL) from floor_day; door counters from zone_traffic_hourly (status ok only); bootstrap 95% intervals
- **Result:** During closure: -31.2% (95% CI -34.5% to -27.9%); after reopening: -4.5% (-7.2% to -1.9%) vs before. Door-counter entries: -31.9% during, -6.1% after. Estimated lost coin-in $1,613,707 during the closure (about $154,432 theo) and $422,984 since (about $40,480 theo).
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Floor-wide demand swings cancel in the daily ratio. Machine mix: per-machine coin-in, and B40's machine count barely changed.
- **Limitation:** Assumes control zones were unaffected by the closure; some B40 players may have moved to them, which would overstate the effect slightly. The bootstrap treats days as independent, so intervals are somewhat too narrow.

### Q2-bank51: The new 8-machine Center Pit bank (B-51, opened Mar 24) is the floor's busiest bank, and its effect on the rest of the pit is small.

- **Table / query:** floor_day: rest-of-pit coin-in per machine relative to control zones, 4 weeks before vs after Mar 24; B-51 per machine relative to the rest of the pit
- **Result:** Rest of pit vs control: +0.4% (95% CI -1.5% to +2.2%). B-51 earned 1.50x the rest of the pit per machine in its first 4 weeks and 1.56x in its last 4.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Novelty: compared the first and last four weeks of the new bank.
- **Limitation:** Four-week windows are short; the basketball tournament falls in the 'before' window.

### Q2-weather: Weather explains little of the day-to-day demand.

- **Table / query:** Ordinary-day coin-in relative to its weekday mean vs NOAA daily max temperature and rain (real)
- **Result:** Correlation with max temperature -0.08; rainy days (>0.5 mm, n=5) differ by -7.4%.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Season: temperature rises steadily through the window, so its correlation also carries the seasonal trend.
- **Limitation:** Few rainy days in Las Vegas; calendar dates, not gaming dates.

## Q3 Downtime

### Q3-total: Downtime removed 5,021 machine-hours (0.38% of scheduled time), worth about $101,227 in expected house win (theo) before substitution.

- **Table / query:** machine_hour (3600 - available_seconds on active hours), valued at each machine's theo per available hour for the same local hour and weekday/weekend; causes from operations
- **Result:** Fault repairs: 4,934 h, $100,064. install: 60 h; move: 12 h; scheduled_maintenance: 15 h. Matches machine_day downtime exactly (5,021 h).
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Substitution: in outage hours, same-bank neighbours earned +3.3% vs their own norm, recovering about 18% of the gross loss.
- **Limitation:** Theo is an expectation; actual lost house win depends on luck. Substitution is measured only within the same bank.

### Q3-when: Downtime cost the most in 2025-05 and on the swing shift; an hour of downtime is worth $28.29 on the swing shift vs $15.28 on the grave shift.

- **Table / query:** down machine-hours grouped by month and by shift (day 06-14, swing 14-22, grave 22-06 local)
- **Result:** 2025-01: 867 h, $18,617; 2025-02: 772 h, $15,950; 2025-03: 979 h, $19,400; 2025-04: 755 h, $15,829; 2025-05: 1,017 h, $19,657; 2025-06: 630 h, $11,774
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Volume of faults vs speed of repair: repair duration is analysed separately against staffing.
- **Limitation:** Shift boundaries are approximate; roster shifts overlap by one hour.

### Q3-manufacturer: Ridgeline machines fail 2.9x as often as the other manufacturers.

- **Table / query:** operations fault_repair tickets per machine-month on the floor, by manufacturer (floor_day exposure)
- **Result:** Ridgeline: 1.21 faults per machine-month, 29.2 h, $589 theo lost and $1,259 direct repair cost per machine; Northwind: 0.50 faults per machine-month, 12.8 h, $253 theo lost and $540 direct repair cost per machine; Halcyon: 0.33 faults per machine-month, 7.8 h, $166 theo lost and $348 direct repair cost per machine
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Heavier use: per 1,000 occupied hours the gap remains (Ridgeline 4.62, Northwind 1.87, Halcyon 1.31).
- **Limitation:** Fault counts include repeat faults on the same machines (see repeat-fault list).

### Q3-staffing: Repairs take longer when the slot-tech shift is short-staffed.

- **Table / query:** Each fault joined to the slot_tech roster for its gaming date and shift (shift_roster); bootstrap difference in mean hours, short vs fully staffed
- **Result:** Mean repair time 5.21 h short-staffed (n=118) vs 3.97 h fully staffed (n=1089): +1.25 h (95% CI +0.62 to +1.91). Time to start work: +1.22 h (+0.71 to +1.74). Short-staffed shift-days: 2025-04 day 17, 2025-05 day 25, 2025-05 swing 14
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Fault mix: short-staffed months also had different fault volumes; compare within shift in the dashboard table (repair_by_staff).
- **Limitation:** Observational; short staffing clusters in April-May, so seasonal effects are not fully separated.

## Q4 Tracked player outcomes

### Q4-outcomes: Tracked players wagered $163,804,870, got back $148,219,409 and netted $-15,585,461; 16.1% of those who gambled finished the six months ahead.

- **Table / query:** player_period summed per player (loyalty-card play only)
- **Result:** 113,890 card holders, 96,559 with carded gaming (17,331 used the card only for food and drink); hold 9.51% vs theo 9.45%; combined luck residual $-100,440. Ahead share falls from 34% in the lightest theo decile to 10% in the heaviest.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Luck vs skill: net = -theo + luck; ahead players are those whose luck exceeded their theo, which becomes rarer the more they play.
- **Limitation:** Uncarded play (about a third of coin-in) is invisible at the person level.

### Q4-concentration: Results are highly concentrated: the top 1% of players produce 23% of tracked theo and the top 10% produce 66%.

- **Table / query:** Lorenz curves and Gini coefficients on per-player theo and losses (player_period)
- **Result:** Gini theo 0.75, losses 0.73, coin-in 0.75. Top 1% of losses: 20%; top 10%: 59%.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Short observation: many players visit once; concentration over a longer window could differ.
- **Limitation:** Tracked guests only; a guest playing partly uncarded looks lighter than they are.

### Q4-locals: Local players are 7% of card holders but produce 58% of tracked theo.

- **Table / query:** player_period grouped by home_region
- **Result:** Domestic: 38% of players, 17% of theo, 1.2 visits, $-61 net per player; International: 14% of players, 6% of theo, 1.2 visits, $-60 net per player; Local: 7% of players, 58% of theo, 27.7 visits, $-1,123 net per player; Regional: 41% of players, 19% of theo, 1.2 visits, $-65 net per player
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Tier: locals are also over-represented in Gold and Platinum, so tier and residence overlap.
- **Limitation:** Home region is coarse and self-reported at enrolment.

### Q4-heavy-loss: The heaviest-loss 1% of players lose far more per visit and are a group for protective outreach, not for more marketing.

- **Table / query:** Players at or below the 1st percentile of net vs everyone else (player_period)
- **Result:** Heaviest-loss 1% (n=1139): mean net $-3,651, mean theo $2,765, 63.1 visits; others: mean net $-101, 2.5 visits.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Bad luck vs volume: the tail's mean luck residual is $-887 against mean theo $2,765, so volume of play, not only luck, drives it.
- **Limitation:** Synthetic guests; no information on affordability. Any outreach should be opt-in.

### Q4-reconciliation: Player tables reconcile exactly with the carded meters; they cover about two-thirds of all play.

- **Table / query:** machine_day carded_coin_in_cents vs player_period tracked_coin_in_usd by month
- **Result:** Maximum monthly gap $0.00; carded share 64.8%-66.8%.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Data error: the exact match rules out missing carded sessions.
- **Limitation:** Uncarded guests can win as a group, so carded and total house win move differently.

## Q5 Comp program

### Q5-change: At the start of gaming day May 1, 2025, the points needed per comp dollar fell by about half in every tier.

- **Table / query:** loyalty_ledger redemptions: points / face value, by tier, before and after
- **Result:** Last old-rate redemption 2025-05-01 10:59:58 UTC; first new-rate redemption 2025-05-01 13:28:22 UTC. Base: 15.0 to 7.0 points per $; Gold: 12.0 to 5.6 points per $; Platinum: 9.0 to 4.2 points per $. Segmented regression level change -6.31 points per $.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Item mix: the change appears in every tier and every benefit at once, not as a drift.
- **Limitation:** The data show the change, not the policy reason for it.

### Q5-cost: The comp program cost $190,669 over six months (1.23% of tracked theo). Cheaper redemptions did not produce a measurable jump in comp cost or play beyond the existing upward trend.

- **Table / query:** Daily interrupted time series (statsmodels OLS: trend + post + post-trend + weekday + event, Newey-West errors) on ledger comp cost, comp cost / carded theo, redemptions, carded coin-in
- **Result:** Comp cost level change -8/day (-0.8%, 95% CI -81 to +65, p=0.82); reinvestment +0.01 pp (p=0.58); redemptions -3.4/day (p=0.77); carded coin-in -2.0% (p=0.44). Monthly reinvestment: 2025-01 1.11%, 2025-02 1.20%, 2025-03 1.22%, 2025-04 1.24%, 2025-05 1.30%, 2025-06 1.32%.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Pre-existing trend: reinvestment was already rising (+0.04 pp per 30 days before May); the model projects that trend forward. Seasonality: weekday and event controls; June's Locals Week is an event day.
- **Limitation:** Two months after the change; a small effect could be missed. One floor-wide change with no control group, which is why a pilot with a control group is the better test.

### Q5-liability: The change roughly doubled what this year's unredeemed points are worth to guests.

- **Table / query:** loyalty_ledger: points awarded minus redeemed through Jun 30, valued at the old and new points-per-dollar rates and the observed economic-cost-to-face ratio
- **Result:** 156,444,221 unredeemed points = $11,955,771 face ($3,338,851 cost) at the old rate vs $27,072,162 face ($7,560,357 cost) at the new rate.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Redemption behaviour: redemptions per visit did not jump, so guests are not yet cashing in the cheaper points; the exposure is latent.
- **Limitation:** Balances reset each year, so unredeemed points may expire; this is exposure, not a booked cost.

## Model: weekday factors x level index

### M-validation: On a frozen May-June holdout, weekday factors times a training-mean level cut daily error to $53,457 MAE, 22% below the weekday-mean baseline ($68,699) and 22% below the previous Holt-Winters model ($68,227). The method April's validation picked (local level with damped drift) also beat both, at $61,758, but followed April's rise too closely.

- **Table / query:** property_day coin-in per scheduled hour; log-rate OLS on weekday + month over ordinary days for the weekly shape; level index carried forward four ways (training mean, last 28 days, ETS local level, ETS damped drift); chosen on Jan-Mar vs April MAE; refit on Jan-Apr and a 61-day forecast frozen to backend/output/forecast_frozen_holdout.csv before scoring
- **Result:** MAE (bias): Training-mean level $53,457 (+11,107); Last-28-day level $63,952 (+35,545); Local level $61,719 (+31,502); Local level with damped drift $61,758 (+31,574); Weekday mean, all days (guide baseline) $68,699 (+39,286); Weekday mean, ordinary days $53,635 (+11,181); Constant mean $218,258 (+28,937); Holt-Winters (previous model) $68,227 (+39,050). April MAE: Local level with damped drift $37,925; Local level $37,998; Last-28-day level $39,098; Training-mean level $47,791. Training-mean level 80% interval covered 70% of days (76% of ordinary days), 95% covered 84% (90%); event and holiday days MAE $96,200 vs ordinary days $42,989. Weekday factors (Jun 30 fit): Mon 0.90, Tue 0.82, Wed 0.86, Thu 0.96, Fri 1.20, Sat 1.32, Sun 1.05.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** June softening vs a contaminated baseline: the weekday mean of ordinary days alone scores $53,635 (bias +11,181) against the guide baseline's $68,699 (+39,286), so most of the old over-forecast came from averaging event days into the baseline. Recent-level methods: the level index rose +2.4% in April over Jan-Mar, then June ran -3.9% below Jan-Apr, so methods that track the latest level over-forecast June. Shape stability: weekday factors moved at most 0.009 between the Mar 31, Apr 30 and Jun 30 fits. Leakage: no event term; Gilded Ace Locals Appreciation Week was announced May 26, after the Apr 30 origin, so the previous event-adjusted forecast used information it could not have had.
- **Limitation:** Two out-of-sample windows (30 and 61 days). The weekly shape does most of the work: the training-mean level is within $178 of a plain ordinary-day weekday mean. The bands come from ordinary-day spread and are too narrow once the level moves. One winter-to-summer pass cannot identify a yearly season; events are not forecast.

### M-decision: The July forecast (training-mean level, lowest error across April and May-June) puts July coin-in at $39,970,506; if June's level persists it is $38,617,927 (-3.4%), which sets the low case for every July-scaled benefit in the budget.

- **Table / query:** July 1-30: Jun 30 weekday factors x level forecast x 24 hours; June-level scenario from the last 28 days; x floor theo hold x each shift's share of coin-in (machine_hour)
- **Result:** $1,332,350/day; weekend days average $1,572,091 vs weekdays $1,245,172. Pooled out-of-sample MAE: Training-mean level $51,589; Local level $53,899; Local level with damped drift $53,901; Last-28-day level $55,759. July 4 band widened by +/-10.4%.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Holiday uplift: no federal holiday in the data lifted coin-in (New Year's Day (Wed) -9.5%; Martin Luther King Jr. Day (Mon) +3.7%; Washington's Birthday (Mon) +0.4%; Memorial Day (Mon) +0.4%; Juneteenth (Thu) -3.6%), so July 4 gets no uplift, only a wider band. Method choice: April alone picked the local level with damped drift; pooling both windows decides the July method.
- **Limitation:** July is outside the observed window, and summer seasonality cannot be estimated from one season; the July 4 band is a judgement from five holidays, none on a Friday.

## Q6 Budget

### Q6-portfolio: Fund 3 action types for $19,800 and keep $80,200: Machine inspection and paytable verification x1 ($1,200); Relocate one machine x2 ($3,600); Property promotion weekend x1 ($15,000).

- **Table / query:** analysis/budget.py: catalogue cost formula, benefits from Q1-Q5 evidence and the July forecast (weekday factors x level), low/mid/high scenarios; funded when the mid-case net benefit is positive
- **Result:** Expected net benefit (mid) $56,235, range $11,631 to $87,721. Not funded (mid-case net): A-TECH-SHIFT -10,699; A-PREVENTIVE -5,608; A-WAYFINDING -4,270; A-COMP-PILOT -4,000; A-COMP-CHANGE -1,500; A-MOVE-BANK -9,500; A-SURVEY -3,500; A-REPLACE-BUY -220,000; A-REPLACE-LEASE -19,930.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** Spending on causes the data do not support: replacements (game mix and location, not cabinets), technicians (staffing is back to plan) and maintenance (pays back after July).
- **Limitation:** Benefits are expectations, mostly theo; action costs are fictional catalogue inputs and no uplift is guaranteed. July-scaled low cases assume June's level persists (-3.4% vs the forecast).

### Q6-no-replacement: Do not replace the manager's bottom 10.

- **Table / query:** Q1 waterfall and Q6 pricing of A-REPLACE-BUY and A-REPLACE-LEASE
- **Result:** Buying all ten costs $220,000, more than double the budget; 2 are already retired; leasing replacements for the other 8 costs $27,040 and loses money in the mid case.
- **Alternative explanation tested:** That the machines are genuinely broken: their hit rates match game math (except M-1158, which is for inspection) and availability is normal.
- **Limitation:** A new title could still out-earn an old one; the evidence says location and game settings dominate.
